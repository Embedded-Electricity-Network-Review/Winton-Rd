Embedded Electricity Network Review

Information for Owners – 5/5A Winton Road, Malvern East

A quick guide to the decision

What are owners being asked to vote on?

Whether the Owners Corporation should terminate its existing embedded electricity supply agreement.

Why is this being proposed?

The Committee has reviewed the existing arrangement over a number of years and considers that the Owners Corporation should have greater control and choice over its electricity supply arrangements.

If the existing agreement is lawfully terminated, the Owners Corporation can consider alternative providers and seek competitive rates and terms for the entire complex — benefiting both owners and tenants.

Does this vote appoint a new provider?

No. The resolution only concerns termination of the existing agreement. Origin Energy has been identified as the Committee's preferred replacement provider, but any appointment would be a separate decision.

Why is a Special Resolution required?

The existing agreement includes a requirement for a Special Resolution to approve its termination. The Committee's position, based on legal advice, is that this requirement is an unfair and void contractual term. The Committee has nevertheless put the Special Resolution to owners.

What happens if the resolution passes?

The Committee can proceed with the next steps to terminate the existing agreement and, if it is lawfully terminated, consider appointing a replacement provider.

What happens if it does not pass?

The existing agreement remains in place unless and until it is otherwise brought to an end. The Committee will retain the ability to consider and pursue other legal options available to the Owners Corporation.

What has happened so far?

The Committee issued a notice of termination. After more than 30 days without a response, the Owners Corporation engaged legal representatives and issued a Letter of Demand. A response was subsequently received but did not provide a meaningful response to the matters raised, requiring further legal communication. The matter was also referred to EWOV, which has assigned an investigator.

Voting closes: Thursday 13 August 2026 at 5 PM.

In summary

Owners are being asked to approve a Special Resolution to terminate the property's existing embedded electricity supply agreement.

The resolution:

  • concerns termination only;
  • does not appoint a new electricity provider;
  • is being put to owners because the existing agreement includes a requirement for a Special Resolution to approve termination; and
  • provides the Owners Corporation with the opportunity to proceed with termination and consider alternative electricity supply arrangements.

The Committee's objective is to have greater control and choice over the electricity provider for the entire complex, allowing the Owners Corporation to consider competitive rates and terms and seek opportunities to reduce electricity costs for owners and tenants.

The Committee has taken steps to terminate the existing agreement, including issuing a Notice of Termination, pursuing the matter through legal representatives after more than 30 days without a response, and referring the matter to the Energy and Water Ombudsman Victoria (EWOV).

The Committee's current position, based on legal advice, is that the contractual requirement for a Special Resolution is an unfair and void term, and that the six-month written notice requirement was satisfied by the Notice of Termination issued by the Owners Corporation.

The resolution does not determine those legal issues. If necessary, the Committee may pursue further legal options on behalf of the Owners Corporation.

The wording on the ballot paper is the wording that governs the vote. Owners should read the notice of meeting and ballot before voting.

What owners are being asked to approve

The property is supplied through an embedded electricity network under an agreement with Shine Power.

Owners are being asked to approve a Special Resolution concerning termination of that agreement.

The resolution put to owners

Should the Owners Corporation terminate its existing embedded electricity supply agreement?

The wording on the ballot paper is the wording that governs the vote. Owners should read the ballot wording and notice of meeting before voting.

What the resolution does
The resolution seeks the approval required under the existing agreement for the Owners Corporation to proceed with termination.
What the resolution does not do
The resolution does not appoint a new electricity provider. If the resolution is passed and the existing agreement is lawfully terminated, the Committee can then proceed with the appointment of a replacement provider under its existing powers.

Why the review was undertaken

Over a number of years, both the previous Committee and the current Committee have considered the operation of the property's embedded electricity network.

The review has considered:

  • the value and operation of the existing arrangement;
  • pricing and billing;
  • communication with the provider and its responsiveness to the Owners Corporation;
  • the long-term operation of the embedded network; and
  • the operation of the property's solar generation and the financial benefits associated with that generation.

The embedded network has continued to provide electricity to the property. The review has therefore focused on the contractual, financial and governance aspects of the arrangement rather than continuity of supply.

What the Committee is seeking to achieve

The Committee's objective is to give the Owners Corporation greater control and choice over its electricity supply arrangements.

If the existing agreement is lawfully terminated, the Owners Corporation can consider alternative providers and compare:

  • electricity rates;
  • pricing structures;
  • contractual terms;
  • service arrangements; and
  • the overall value offered to the complex.

This creates an opportunity to seek competitive electricity costs for the entire complex, including both owner-occupied and tenanted lots.

The Committee does not represent that a particular saving or rate is guaranteed. Any future costs or benefits will depend on the provider and arrangements ultimately selected.

Proposed replacement provider

The Committee has identified Origin Energy as its preferred replacement provider.

Origin currently manages the property's embedded hot water service and has offered discounts on that basis.

Importantly, Origin is not being appointed by this vote.

Any appointment of a replacement provider would be a separate step following lawful termination of the existing agreement.

Steps taken to date

The Committee has taken a number of steps concerning termination of the existing agreement.

Notice of termination

The Committee issued a formal Notice of Termination to the provider.

No response was received for more than 30 days.

Legal correspondence

Following the absence of a response, the Owners Corporation engaged legal representatives to pursue the matter.

A Letter of Demand was issued.

A response was subsequently received from the provider. The response did not provide a meaningful response to the matters raised and further legal communication was required.

Energy and Water Ombudsman Victoria

The matter was also referred to the Energy and Water Ombudsman Victoria (EWOV).

EWOV contacted the provider seeking a response and has assigned an investigator to the matter.

Those enquiries are continuing. The outcome is not yet known.

The Committee will update owners if further material information becomes available.

The agreement and notice period

The existing agreement provides for six months' written notice before the end of the relevant term.

It also contains an automatic renewal provision for a further five-year term.

The six-month notice window for the current term closed on 26 June 2026.

The Owners Corporation issued its Notice of Termination during the relevant notice period.

The Owners Corporation has obtained legal advice concerning the notice period, renewal provisions and the requirement for a Special Resolution.

The Committee's position, based on that advice, is outlined below.

The Committee's legal position

The Committee has been legally advised in relation to a number of provisions of the existing agreement.

Based on that advice, the Committee's position is that several provisions are unfair contractual terms and are therefore void and unenforceable.

These include provisions concerning:

  • automatic renewal of the agreement;
  • the requirement for a Special Resolution before the Owners Corporation can give notice of termination;
  • the 90-day notice provision applying to Shine's termination without cause; and
  • Shine's discretion not to provide services during an alleged default.

The Committee's position also takes into account the allocation of ownership, maintenance and insurance responsibilities under the agreement, including the Owners Corporation's responsibilities concerning the embedded network infrastructure and Shine's equipment.

The Special Resolution requirement

The Committee's position is that the requirement in clause 11.2(b)(i) for a Special Resolution before notice of termination can be given is an unfair and void contractual term.

The Committee considers that this requirement operates to restrict the Owners Corporation's ability to terminate the agreement.

The Committee's position is also that clause 11.2(b) requires six months' written notice and that the Notice of Termination issued by the Owners Corporation provided that notice.

Important qualification

This is the Committee's position based on legal advice received by the Owners Corporation.

It is not presented as a determination by a court or tribunal.

If the provider disputes the Owners Corporation's position, the matter may require further legal action or determination by an appropriate court or tribunal.

The relevant legal correspondence and advice are available to lot owners on request from the Owners Corporation Manager.

What each voting outcome means

If the resolution is passed

  • The Owners Corporation will have passed the Special Resolution sought in relation to the existing agreement.
  • The Committee can proceed with the next steps concerning termination.
  • If the agreement is lawfully terminated, the Committee can consider and appoint a replacement provider under its existing powers.
  • The resolution itself does not appoint Origin Energy or any other provider.
  • The existing provider may dispute the termination. If that occurs, the Owners Corporation may need to respond and may incur further legal costs.
  • The Committee's intention is for electricity supply to continue throughout any changeover.

If the resolution is not passed

  • The Special Resolution referred to in clause 11.2(b)(i) of the existing agreement will not have been obtained.
  • The existing agreement would remain in place unless and until the Owners Corporation takes further action to terminate it, or the agreement is otherwise brought to an end.
  • The Committee would not proceed with the appointment of a replacement provider on the basis of this resolution.
  • A vote against the resolution would not prevent the Owners Corporation from considering or pursuing other legal options in relation to the existing agreement.
  • The Committee's position remains that the Special Resolution requirement is an unfair and void contractual term and that the six-month written notice requirement was satisfied by the Notice of Termination.
  • The Committee will consider the legal options available to the Owners Corporation if the resolution is not passed.

Questions and answers

  1. What is an embedded network?

    An embedded electricity network is a private electricity network within a building or complex. Electricity is supplied to the property through a central connection and distributed to individual lots through the embedded network.

    The property at 5/5A Winton Road operates under an embedded electricity arrangement of this kind.
