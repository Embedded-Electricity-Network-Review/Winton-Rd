What owners are being asked to approve

The property is supplied through an embedded electricity network under an agreement with Shine Power. Owners are being asked to approve a Special Resolution to terminate that agreement.

The resolution put to owners

Should the owners corporation terminate its embedded electricity supply agreement?

What the resolution does
Seeks approval from owners to enable the Owners Corporation to terminate the existing agreement in accordance with its contractual requirements.
What the resolution does not do
It does not appoint a new electricity provider. If the resolution is passed and the agreement is lawfully terminated, the Committee will then be able to proceed with the appointment of a replacement provider under its existing powers.

Why owners are being asked to vote

Appointing a supplier is a decision within the Committee's powers. Terminating the existing agreement is not. The agreement includes a contractual requirement that it can only be terminated following approval by a Special Resolution of the Owners Corporation. It is because of that requirement that owners are being asked to vote.

Background to the review

Over a number of years, both the previous Committee and the current Committee have undertaken an extensive review of the existing embedded network arrangements.

The review considered:

  • the overall value provided to owners and residents;
  • the transparency of pricing and billing;
  • communication with the provider, and its responsiveness to the Owners Corporation;
  • the long-term operation of the embedded network; and
  • whether the financial benefits associated with the property's solar generation were being appropriately realised for owners and residents.

Supply has remained reliable

The Committee acknowledges that the embedded electricity network has continued to provide a reliable electricity supply to the property. The review was therefore not directed at continuity of supply, but at broader governance considerations.

The Committee's decision

Having considered these matters over an extended period, the Committee resolved to seek termination of the existing agreement and, if the agreement is lawfully terminated, to appoint an alternative embedded electricity provider.

The Committee has identified Origin Energy as its preferred replacement provider. Origin manages the property's existing embedded hot water service, and has offered discounts on that basis. As set out above, the resolution itself does not appoint Origin or any other provider.

The notice period and the term of the agreement

The agreement provides that notice of termination must be given at least six months before the end of the term, and that it otherwise renews automatically for a further five years. The notice window for the current term closed on [date TBC], before this vote could be held.

The Owners Corporation has obtained legal advice on the effect of that window closing and on the provider's failure to respond. On that advice, the Committee is putting the resolution to owners now. What each outcome means

If the resolution is passed

  • The Owners Corporation has the approval the agreement requires in order to terminate it.
  • If the agreement is lawfully terminated, the Committee can then appoint a replacement provider under its existing powers.
  • The provider may dispute the termination. If it does, the Owners Corporation would need to respond, which may involve further legal cost.
  • Electricity supply to lots continues throughout any changeover. No action is required inside individual lots.

If the resolution is not passed

  • The condition the agreement sets for termination is not met, and the agreement remains in force.
  • It continues on its existing terms for the balance of the five-year renewal term.
  • A replacement provider cannot be appointed.
  • The next opportunity to terminate under the agreement's own terms would be the six-month window before the end of that term, on [date TBC].

How the vote works

Voting is conducted under the Owners Corporations Act 2006 (Vic) and the Owners Corporation's rules. The following is a general summary. The notice of meeting is the authoritative document.

  • A Special Resolution is passed where at least 75% of the total votes of the Owners Corporation are cast in favour.
  • The 75% is measured against every lot in the plan, not only the lots that return a ballot. An unreturned ballot counts toward the total but not toward the resolution.
  • Where fewer than half of the lots vote but 75% of the votes cast are in favour, the Act provides for an interim special resolution, which may take effect after a further period unless a sufficient number of lot owners object within it. Confirm with the Owners Corporation Manager whether this applies to your vote.
  • Fees and charges owing on a lot can affect entitlement to vote.
  • You may vote in person, by proxy, or by the ballot method described in the notice of meeting.

Questions and answers

  1. What is an embedded network?

    An embedded network is a private electricity network within a building. The Owners Corporation buys electricity at a single connection point and on-sells it to lots through private meters, rather than each lot connecting separately to a grid retailer. The arrangement at this property is an embedded network of that kind.

  2. Does this resolution appoint a new provider?

    No. It seeks approval to enable the Owners Corporation to terminate the existing agreement. Appointing a replacement provider is a separate step, taken by the Committee under its existing powers, and only if the agreement is lawfully terminated.

  3. Has the deadline to terminate already passed?

    The six-month notice window for the current term closed on [date TBC], before the vote could be held. The Owners Corporation issued its notice of termination and pursued it through its legal representatives and the Ombudsman during that period, but received no response.

  4. If the window has closed, what does a vote in favour achieve?

    This is the question on which the Owners Corporation obtained legal advice. A resolution in favour records the Owners Corporation's approval to terminate and authorises the Committee to act on it. What follows depends on that advice and on whether the provider responds. The full advice is available from the Owners Corporation Manager, and owners who want certainty on this point should read it before voting.

  5. Why is a vote needed if the provider will not respond?

    Termination must be valid on the agreement's own terms whether or not the other party engages. Passing the resolution means the Owners Corporation has satisfied the condition the agreement imposes.

  6. Will electricity supply be interrupted?

    A change of embedded network provider is an administrative and billing change. Supply to lots is expected to continue throughout. If any work at the meter board required a brief interruption, owners and residents would be given advance notice by the incoming provider.

  7. Is anything required inside my lot?

    No. If the provider changes, owners and residents would receive account setup information from the incoming provider directly. Final billing with the outgoing provider would be handled through the Owners Corporation Manager.

  8. Can I choose my own retailer instead?

    Customers in embedded networks in Victoria generally retain a right to seek supply from an external retailer, but in practice this depends on metering arrangements and on a retailer being willing to offer a contract at the site. It is a separate question from this resolution.

  9. What will this cost the Owners Corporation?

    Legal costs incurred to date are recorded in the Owners Corporation's accounts and are available for inspection. If the resolution is passed and the provider disputes the termination, further costs may be incurred. A written comparison of current and proposed rates is issued with the meeting papers so that owners can assess the supply cost difference themselves.

  10. The Committee says the contract terms are unfair. Has that been determined?

    No. That is the Committee's characterisation of the termination, notice and renewal provisions. It has not been determined by a court or tribunal, and this vote does not decide it.

  11. I have a billing complaint about my own account. Where does that go?

    Individual billing disputes are separate from this resolution. In Victoria they can be raised with the Energy and Water Ombudsman (Victoria), which handles complaints from customers of embedded networks at no cost to the customer. Raising a complaint does not affect your vote.
