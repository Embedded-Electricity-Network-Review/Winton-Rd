Where a statement represents the Committee's view or a position put forward by the Owners Corporation's legal representatives, it is identified as such. The page is not intended to advocate for or against the resolution.

What owners are being asked to approve

The property is supplied through an embedded electricity network under an agreement with Shine Power. Owners are being asked to approve a Special Resolution concerning termination of that agreement.

The resolution put to owners

Should the Owners Corporation terminate its existing embedded electricity supply agreement?

Information currently available to the Committee indicates that individual lot owners were not aware of the agreement before entering into their contracts of sale.

The Committee has also not identified a clear record showing that the agreement was disclosed to the Owners Corporation for its records at that time.

The agreement creates an ongoing contractual arrangement concerning electricity supply to the property. The current resolution gives owners the opportunity to consider whether the Owners Corporation should seek to terminate that arrangement.

The wording that appears on your ballot paper is the wording that governs the vote. Owners should read the ballot wording and notice of meeting before voting.

What the resolution does
The resolution seeks the approval required under the existing agreement for the Owners Corporation to proceed with termination.
What the resolution does not do
The resolution does not appoint a new electricity provider. If the resolution is passed and the existing agreement is lawfully terminated, the Committee can then proceed with the appointment of a replacement provider under its existing powers.

Why owners are being asked to vote

The existing agreement includes a contractual requirement for a Special Resolution to approve its termination.

The appointment of an electricity provider is a separate matter from termination of the existing agreement. The Special Resolution is therefore being put to owners because of the requirement contained in the existing agreement.

Background to the review

Over a number of years, both the previous Committee and the current Committee have considered the operation of the property's embedded electricity network.

The review has considered:

  • the value and operation of the existing arrangement;
  • pricing and billing;
  • communication with the provider and its responsiveness to the Owners Corporation;
  • the long-term operation of the embedded network; and
  • the operation of the property's solar generation and the financial benefits associated with that generation.

Supply has remained reliable

The Committee acknowledges that the embedded electricity network has continued to provide electricity to the property.

The review has therefore focused on the contractual, financial and governance aspects of the arrangement, rather than on the continuity of electricity supply.

Why is the Owners Corporation seeking greater choice?

The purpose of the review is to give the Owners Corporation greater control over its electricity supply arrangements.

If the existing agreement is lawfully terminated, the Owners Corporation would be able to consider alternative providers and compare rates, terms and service arrangements.

This creates an opportunity to seek competitive electricity costs for the entire complex, including arrangements that benefit both owners and tenants.

The Committee does not represent that a particular saving or rate is guaranteed. Any future rates, costs or benefits would depend on the provider and arrangements ultimately selected.

The proposed replacement provider

The Committee has identified Origin Energy as its preferred replacement provider.

Origin currently manages the property's embedded hot water service and has offered discounts on that basis.

The proposed appointment of Origin is not part of the Special Resolution. No replacement provider is appointed by the resolution itself.

Steps taken to date

The Committee has taken a number of steps concerning termination of the existing agreement.

The relevant correspondence and supporting documents are held by the Owners Corporation Manager and are available to lot owners on request.

Notice of termination

The Committee issued a formal notice of termination to the provider.

No response was received for more than 30 days.

Legal correspondence

Following the absence of a response, the Owners Corporation engaged legal representatives to pursue the matter.

The legal representatives issued a Letter of Demand.

A response was subsequently received from the provider. The Owners Corporation's legal representatives considered that the response did not provide a meaningful response to the matters raised, and further legal communication was required.

Energy and Water Ombudsman Victoria

The matter was also referred to the Energy and Water Ombudsman Victoria (EWOV).

EWOV contacted the provider seeking a response and has assigned an investigator to the matter.

The enquiries are continuing and the outcome is not yet known.

The Committee will update owners if further material information is received.

The notice period and term of the agreement

The existing agreement provides for a six-month written notice period before the end of the relevant term.

It also contains an automatic renewal provision for a further five-year term.

The six-month notice window for the current term closed on 26 June 2026.

The Owners Corporation obtained legal advice concerning the notice period, the renewal provisions and the contractual requirement for a Special Resolution.

The legal advice is available to lot owners on request from the Owners Corporation Manager.

The legal position concerning these provisions is outlined below.

Legal position raised by the Owners Corporation

The Owners Corporation's legal representatives have raised concerns with Shine Power regarding several provisions of the existing agreement.

In correspondence to Shine, the Owners Corporation's lawyers stated that they consider particular provisions of the agreement to be unfair and therefore void and unenforceable.

The provisions identified include:

  • Clause 11.2, concerning automatic renewal of the agreement;
  • Clause 11.2(b)(i), which requires the Owners Corporation to pass a Special Resolution before giving notice;
  • Clause 12.3, concerning 90 days' notice for Shine to terminate without cause; and
  • Clause 12.2, concerning Shine's discretion not to provide services during an alleged default.

The Owners Corporation's legal representatives have also disputed Shine's position that the renewal term provides the tenure certainty necessary to justify and recover its capital investment.

Their correspondence refers to provisions of the agreement under which they state that:

  • the Owners Corporation owns the embedded network infrastructure;
  • the Owners Corporation maintains that infrastructure at its own cost;
  • the Owners Corporation pays for maintenance and upgrades to Shine's equipment; and
  • the Owners Corporation insures Shine's equipment.

The legal correspondence refers to section 24(4) of the Australian Consumer Law and to previous Federal Court decisions concerning unfair terms in standard-form contracts.

The Special Resolution precondition

The Owners Corporation's legal representatives have specifically challenged the requirement in clause 11.2(b)(i) for a Special Resolution before notice of termination can be given.

They have advised that they consider this requirement to be an unfair and void contractual term, and have characterised it as a term that operates to restrict an Owners Corporation's ability to terminate the agreement.

They have also advised that clause 11.2(b) requires six months' written notice and that the Notice of Termination issued by the Owners Corporation provided that notice.

Important qualification

The statements in this section describe the legal position put forward by the Owners Corporation's legal representatives.

They are not presented as a determination by a court or tribunal.

Whether any particular contractual term is unfair, void or unenforceable is ultimately a matter of law and, if disputed, may require determination by an appropriate court or tribunal.

The full legal correspondence is available to lot owners on request from the Owners Corporation Manager.

What each outcome means

If the resolution is passed

  • The Owners Corporation will have passed the Special Resolution requested under the existing agreement.
  • The Committee can then proceed with the next steps concerning termination, subject to the legal position and the terms of the agreement.
  • If the agreement is lawfully terminated, the Committee can consider and appoint a replacement provider under its existing powers.
  • The resolution itself does not appoint Origin Energy or any other provider.
  • The existing provider may dispute the termination. If that occurs, the Owners Corporation may need to respond and may incur further legal costs.
  • The Committee's intention is for electricity supply to continue throughout any changeover. Any operational requirements associated with a provider change would be communicated to affected owners and residents.

If the resolution is not passed

  • The Special Resolution referred to in clause 11.2(b)(i) of the existing agreement will not have been obtained.
  • The existing agreement would remain in place unless and until the Owners Corporation takes further action to terminate it, or the agreement is otherwise brought to an end.
  • The Committee would not proceed with the appointment of a replacement provider on the basis of this resolution.
  • A vote against the resolution would not prevent the Owners Corporation from considering or pursuing other legal options in relation to the existing agreement.
  • The Owners Corporation's legal representatives have advised that they consider the Special Resolution requirement in clause 11.2(b)(i) to be an unfair and void contractual term.
  • They have also advised that clause 11.2(b) requires six months' written notice and that the Notice of Termination issued by the Owners Corporation provided that notice.
  • If the resolution is not passed, the Committee will consider the legal options available to the Owners Corporation, including whether further legal action is appropriate.

The legal position described above is the position advanced by the Owners Corporation's legal representatives. It has not been determined by a court or tribunal.

How the vote works

Voting is conducted under the Owners Corporations Act 2006 (Vic) and the Owners Corporation's rules.

The notice of meeting and ballot is the authoritative document for this particular vote.

In general:

  • The voting threshold for a Special Resolution is determined under the Act and depends on the voting process being used.
  • Owners should follow the instructions accompanying their ballot.
  • Owners may vote using the method specified in the notice of meeting.
  • Questions about voting entitlement or the ballot process should be directed to the Owners Corporation Manager.

Voting closes: Thursday 13 August 2026 at 5 PM.

Owners are encouraged to review the notice of meeting, ballot wording and supporting documents before voting.

Questions and answers

  1. What is an embedded network?

    An embedded electricity network is a private electricity network within a building or complex. Electricity is supplied to the property through a central connection and distributed to individual lots through the embedded network.

    The property at 5/5A Winton Road operates under an embedded electricity arrangement of this kind.

  2. Does this resolution appoint a new provider?

    No.

    The resolution concerns termination of the existing agreement. If that agreement is lawfully terminated, the Committee can then consider appointing a replacement provider under its existing powers.

  3. Is Origin Energy being appointed by this vote?

    No.

    Origin Energy has been identified by the Committee as its preferred replacement provider. Any appointment would be a separate decision following lawful termination of the existing agreement.

  4. Why is a Special Resolution required?

    The existing agreement includes a contractual requirement for a Special Resolution to approve its termination.

    That contractual requirement is why the matter has been put to owners.

    The Owners Corporation's legal representatives have separately advised that they consider this requirement to be an unfair and void term. That legal position has not been determined by a court or tribunal.

  5. Has the six-month notice window closed?

    Yes. The six-month notice window for the current term closed on 26 June 2026.
